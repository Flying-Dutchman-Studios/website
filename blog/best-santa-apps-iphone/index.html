Best Santa and Christmas apps
for iPhone in 2026

From a personal call from Santa to the NORAD tracker, music and the family wish list. What each app does, what it costs, whether it carries ads, and which ones want a photo of your child.

Calls from Santa

1. Call from Santa — Santa calls, and you decide what he says

This is the only app in this list where the parent builds the call instead of picking a ready-made one. It sounds like a detail, and it is the whole difference between “Santa says your child’s name correctly” and “Santa knows Mila lost a tooth on Tuesday”.

You go through three steps in the parent area. First you choose where you are in the story: Santa is still at the North Pole, Santa has already landed, tonight is Christmas Eve, Santa is on his way back home. That decides what the call is about. Then you fill in your child’s name, with an optional field for how it is pronounced, so Santa says “Mee-la” rather than guessing.

Then you build the content: a compliment, a little task, something that happened this week, the wish list, and a question your child can answer out loud. Every part comes with suggestions you can pick, and every part can be typed yourself instead.

Then you listen to the whole message back. If something is off, you change it and listen again. Only when you are happy do you choose whether the phone rings now, with thirty seconds to get the handset ready, or whether it is set up for later that evening.

The home screen, then step one where you choose the moment in the story, then step two where you set the name, the pronunciation and the compliment.

What it costs: the first three messages are free. After that 3 messages for €1.99, 7 for €2.99 or 15 for €3.99. No subscription, no automatic renewal, no forced ads, and it never asks for a photo of your child.

Best for: parents who want the call to be about their actual child, and anyone with an anxious child who should hear nothing about the naughty list.

2. Portable North Pole

The giant of this category: ten million downloads and a 4.6 average from more than half a million ratings, updated this month. More than forty scenarios, over fifty scenes, bedtime audio stories, and a live “Talk to Santa” with up to ten personalised exchanges, which is the most ambitious thing anyone here is doing.

Personalisation works by choosing. Even the feature called Write Your Own Topic is explained on their own site as “You choose the subject”. The subject, not the words.

Two things to weigh. They publish no price anywhere on their own website; you find out at checkout, and it varies by country. And their store listing says Santa uses your child’s name, age and photo. To their credit, their help centre states they run no third-party advertising.

Price single video €5.99–€9.99, single call €3.99–€9.99, Magic Pass €21.49, Ultimate Pass €39.99 · Ads none stated · Photo of your child yes

3. Video Call Santa

Santa on screen, and on iPhone it is one of the best-rated of the lot: 4.7 from 160,000 ratings. You pick one of 46 pre-recorded episodes and choose a name from a list of more than 7,000.

The developer is open about what it is: “Video Call Santa currently uses pre-recorded Santa videos”, and the Play listing says all video calls are pre-recorded simulations. The recording is yours to keep.

The catch is the name list. Individual names cost $1.99, so an unusual name or a second child is a separate purchase on top of the pack.

Price VIP $14.99, all episodes $9.99, single episode $2.99, single name $1.99 · Ads yes on Android · Rating 4.7 iPhone, 3.5 Play

4. Speak to Santa

Video calls, voice calls and messages, built hard against the feeling of a recording. Their own description: “this is not a simple video recording of Santa, this is a dynamic, varied, and realistic simulated call”. You set your child’s name, age and favourites, and the app assembles from thousands of possible setups.

The pricing is the messiest here. The same purchase appears at two different amounts across their listings, and the separate Pro Edition app costs $39.99 to download while it has not been updated since November 2021. Stick to the free app with an in-app upgrade.

Price yearly $9.99 or $6.99, lifetime $29.99, Pro Edition app $39.99 · Rating 4.5 from 102,000

5. Message from Santa

The widest feature set for the least money: a recorded video, a simulated call, voicemail and a chat, with a yearly plan at $9.99. Five million downloads and a 4.8 on iPhone.

It is also the only one here with ads on both platforms and an in-app purchase literally called “Unlock All Features & Remove Ads”. The videos are a choice of three ready-made ones with your child’s name and photo added, and the calls and messages are, in their own words, “simulated and powered by AI”.

Price Premium+ $9.99 a year, personalised video $6.99–$19.99 · Ads yes · Photo of your child yes

Tracking, photos and the rest

6. NORAD Santa Tracker

Running since 1955, free, and with no catch at all. Put it on around five o’clock on the 24th and let the children check where he is between courses.

It also hands you the only bedtime argument that has ever worked: he will not come while you are awake.

Price free · Ads none · Best for Christmas Eve itself

7. Capture The Magic

Not a call app at all, though it turns up in every Santa roundup. It drops a Santa into a photo of your own hallway, with almost a hundred characters to choose from, and the result on Christmas morning is genuinely convincing to a five-year-old.

Worth knowing before you install: it carries a 16+ age rating, unlike everything else here, it has not been updated since December 2023, and its privacy label lists your photos and videos with third-party advertising among the purposes.

Price free with purchases up to $4.99 · Age 16+ · Updated December 2023

Music and wish lists

8. Christmas music, without another app

Apple Music and Spotify both run Christmas stations from late November, and you almost certainly pay for one of them already.

Download one playlist for offline use before you leave the house. The weakest link in Christmas Eve is the wifi at someone else’s place.

Price free with ads, or your existing subscription

9. Wish lists the whole family can see

Giftster, GiftList and similar apps all do one job: a shared list where relatives can tick something off without the others seeing it.

Ten minutes to set up, and it saves two duplicate presents every single year. Pick whichever one most of the family already has installed.

Price free, with paid extras

Before you install anything

10. What to set up on an iPhone first

Turn on Screen Time and block in-app purchases. Several apps above sell packs between $5 and $20, and a child tapping through a Santa app will find them.

For a child’s account under Family Sharing, switch on “Ask to Buy”, so you get asked first.

And read App Privacy on every listing before you install. Look for one thing above all: does it want a photo of your child? That is a piece of data you do not get back.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Santa app?

For a personal call where you decide what Santa says, Call from Santa is the only one where the parent builds the message. For the biggest production with the most scenarios, Portable North Pole. For Santa on screen, Video Call Santa. For free tracking on Christmas Eve, NORAD.

Are Santa apps free?

All of them are free to download and all of them charge inside the app. Portable North Pole publishes no price at all until checkout, and Speak to Santa lists the same purchase at two different amounts.

Which Santa apps ask for a photo of your child?

Portable North Pole, Message from Santa and Personalized Call from Santa all do, according to their own listings. Call from Santa does not.

What age are Santa call apps for?

Roughly three to nine. Below that a child does not follow the call; above it most children are already working it out for themselves.
